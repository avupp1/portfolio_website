Hi!
I’m Avanthika Vuppala, currently pursuing Aerospace Engineering at Rutgers University. What draws me to this field is the challenge of designing systems that don’t just work in theory, but actually perform in real-world conditions where precision, efficiency, and reliability matter. I’m especially interested in how different engineering components interact and how small design decisions can significantly impact overall system performance.

My main interests include aerospace systems design, propulsion, structural analysis, advanced materials, and robotics, along with growing interests in thermal and energy systems and how engineering principles are applied in real-world environments. I’m particularly fascinated by propulsion systems and how they can be optimized for efficiency and performance. I’m also interested in advanced materials and how they can be used to create lighter, stronger, and more durable aerospace structures.

I’m also very interested in robotics and autonomous systems across aerospace, including applications in satellites, aviation, drones, and space exploration systems. I enjoy learning how robotics can improve automation, control, and reliability in complex engineering environments, as well as how similar concepts can be applied to broader systems such as manufacturing and energy-efficient designs.

I’m working toward a career where I can contribute to the development of innovative and reliable engineering systems, especially in aerospace, while continuing to grow my skills through research, projects, and hands-on experience. I am particularly interested in applying engineering fundamentals to improve system performance, efficiency, and real-world functionality across different industries.

Outside of engineering, I enjoy crocheting, painting, drawing, and trying new cuisines. I also design my own nails with 3D art, paint with oil pastels, and build small 3D structures out of origami. I’ve always been drawn to creating things, and that creativity shapes the way I approach both my hobbies and my engineering work.
